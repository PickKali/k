My main genre is the platforming genre, and over time, Roblox had been pushing for R15 avatars but the general platforming community didn't use it for the simple reason of inconsistent physics.

There were some notable differences with the physics:
1) Animations could avoid "touch events", meaning that a player would evade hit detection if they were using the right animation. 
    * This became a common speedrunning strategy that the **DEFAULT** jump animation would sometimes evade the hit detection of a collidable flat object. Make sure that your flat lava isn't collidable. ;P
        * You could also evade flat lava by using a levitating animation.
2) Avatar scaling allows for people to have different heights, depths, and widths.
3) Various body parts have different masses, meaning that jumps, swimming, and even falling are varied across avatars.
4) Custom animations can allow players to phase through walls, floors, ceilings, or even appear invisible to other players. 

### First Attempt
Back in 2024, when I first started this project, I had an attempt by looking at what had already been done.
It wasn't as hopeful as I thought.

The only real attempt at the time came from a user named MaximumADHD, and this approach worked by welding a R15 avatar to the R6 avatar. This approach somewhat worked, since it eliminated animations as a whole since it used R6 as a base. Even at the time, custom animations wasn't a thing yet. That solved problems 1 and 4. It didn't scale any avatars so taller characters would appear sinking into the ground, and finding an open library for that, problem 2 was solved. I hadn't noticed problem 3 at the time since I had it already go out for user testing.

And from that user testing, I found out that one of the core gameplay aspects of the more difficult obbying side (truss gameplay) behaved differently. That project was shelved for a year since I couldn't identify what was causing it.

5) Trusses don't allow for W-tapping. (Jumping off of a truss by holding W and tapping space at its peak.)

### Roblox's Own Attempt

A year passed and Roblox announced consistent hitboxes as a gameplay enhancement. It actually didn't do anything effective. It welded a part to the player for developers to use for hitboxes in PVP contexts.

It wasn't designed with obbies in mind. Listening for the collision wouldn't work since it hovered above the ground and stretching it to the ground would have the player collide with small steps.

Looking through the settings, I noticed a setting that gave me a lead on how to fix this problem that should've been fixed a long time ago.

### Dandelion

I created a short obby to test around the mechanics, intended to be on the harder half of obbies using the aforementioned mechanics and a little bit more.

I had created my own custom collision script to do what Roblox's collision setting did but modifiable so I could add something like a bounding box to show the player collision. It performed just as well as the 2024 version, but still had the problem of truss mechanics being different.

And the core difference is that it didn't remove any animations of the player. It even worked for the animated heads that were introduced after I started this version!

There was a funny issue where I noticed that I could stick to two stud underhangs of walls, which seemed like an issue with my collision system. Even testing it in the older R6 avatar, I noticed that it still was a thing. I accidentally found a new obby tech. This was actually partially found earlier for a "cheese" method of doing a wallwalk around a cylinder. The bottom part just happened to be 2 studs, but as far as I know, this hadn't been elaborated on.

Because the problem 5 hadn't been solved, it was shelved once more.

### Locking In (The Gate)

At first, I thought the issue was with the mass, so I had the collision have an invisible R6 avatar layout so that the center of mass would be the same, while converting the rest of the avatar to have no mass, including the base collision.

This made the jumps more consistent, so problem 3 was solved. I had to understand how W-tapping worked in order to solve problem 5. In order for the player to jump, the player has to be in a `running` state, which indicates to the player that they can jump vertically instead of away from the truss in the `climbing` state.
